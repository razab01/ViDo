Sample Space||sample space,all outcomes,outcomes of an experiment||The sample space is the set of all possible outcomes of a random experiment, and every event is a subset of it||S = set of all possible outcomes||Events are subsets of the sample space
Axioms of Probability||axioms of probability,probability axioms,kolmogorov axioms||Probability is non-negative, the whole sample space has probability one, and disjoint events add||P(A) >= 0; P(S)=1; P(A union B)=P(A)+P(B) for disjoint A and B||Together the axioms imply every probability lies between 0 and 1
Mutually Exclusive Events||mutually exclusive,mutually exclusive events,disjoint events||Mutually exclusive events cannot occur together, so their intersection has probability zero||P(A and B)=0; P(A or B)=P(A)+P(B)||Mutually exclusive is not the same as independent
Independent Events||independent events,independence,statistical independence||Events are independent when knowing one does not change the probability of the other||P(A given B)=P(A) and P(A and B)=P(A)P(B)||Independence is different from mutual exclusivity
Conditional Probability||conditional probability,given event,probability given B||Conditional probability restricts the sample space to outcomes where the given event happened||P(A given B)=P(A and B)/P(B)||Use this when information about B is already known
Bayes Theorem||bayes theorem,bayes rule,posterior probability||Bayes theorem updates a prior belief using observed evidence||P(A given B)=P(B given A)P(A)/P(B)||Prior times likelihood, normalised, gives the posterior
Random Variable||random variable,discrete random variable,continuous random variable||A random variable maps outcomes of a random experiment to numbers||X maps sample space outcomes to real values||Discrete variables take countable values while continuous variables take intervals
Probability Mass Function||probability mass function,pmf,discrete distribution||A probability mass function gives the probability of each value of a discrete random variable||P(X=x)=p(x); sum of all p(x) equals 1||PMF values are non-negative and sum to one
Probability Density Function||probability density function,pdf,continuous density||A probability density function describes a continuous variable; probabilities come from area under the curve||P(a <= X <= b)=integral from a to b of f(x)dx||Density itself is not a probability but total area is one
Cumulative Distribution Function||cumulative distribution function,cdf||The cumulative distribution function gives the probability that a variable is at most x||F(x)=P(X <= x)||A CDF is non-decreasing and runs from 0 to 1
Expected Value||expected value,expectation,mean of a random variable||Expected value is the weighted long-run average of a random variable||E[X]=sum x times P(X=x) or integral x f(x)dx||Expectation is linear: E[aX+b]=aE[X]+b
Variance||variance,var,spread around the mean||Variance measures average squared spread around the expected value||Var(X)=E[(X-E[X])^2]=E[X^2]-E[X]^2||The square root of variance is standard deviation
Standard Deviation||standard deviation,sd,root variance||Standard deviation is the square root of variance and measures spread in original units||SD(X)=sqrt(Var(X))||A smaller standard deviation means tighter clustering around the mean
Covariance||covariance,joint variation||Covariance measures how two random variables vary together||Cov(X,Y)=E[(X-E[X])(Y-E[Y])]||Positive means they rise together, negative means one falls as the other rises
Correlation||correlation,correlation coefficient,pearson correlation||Correlation measures the strength and direction of a linear relationship, bounded between -1 and 1||r=Cov(X,Y)/(SD(X)SD(Y))||Correlation does not imply causation
Chebyshev Inequality||chebyshev inequality,chebyshev bound||Chebyshev inequality bounds the share of values near the mean for any distribution with finite variance||P(|X-mean| >= k SD) <= 1/k^2||At least 1 minus 1/k squared lies within k standard deviations
Law of Large Numbers||law of large numbers,lln||The law of large numbers says long-run sample averages converge to the expected value||sample mean converges to E[X] as n grows||It explains why relative frequency stabilises near the true probability
Central Limit Theorem||central limit theorem,clt||The central limit theorem says sample means are approximately normal for large samples||sample mean is approximately normal with mean mu and variance sigma squared over n||It justifies normal approximations and z-tests
Bernoulli Distribution||bernoulli distribution,bernoulli trial,single success failure trial||A Bernoulli distribution models one trial with success one and failure zero||P(X=1)=p; P(X=0)=1-p; E[X]=p; Var(X)=p(1-p)||It is the building block of the binomial distribution
Binomial Distribution||binomial distribution,count successes in trials||A binomial distribution counts successes in a fixed number of independent trials with equal success probability||P(X=k)=C(n,k)p^k(1-p)^(n-k)||Mean is np and variance is np(1-p)
Poisson Distribution||poisson distribution,event count in interval||A Poisson distribution counts events in a fixed interval when they occur independently at a constant rate lambda||P(X=k)=e^(-lambda) lambda^k/k!||Mean and variance both equal lambda
Geometric Distribution||geometric distribution,trials until first success||A geometric distribution counts trials until the first success in independent Bernoulli trials||P(X=k)=(1-p)^(k-1)p; E[X]=1/p||It has the memoryless property
Exponential Distribution||exponential distribution,waiting time distribution||An exponential distribution models waiting time between events in a Poisson process with rate lambda||f(x)=lambda e^(-lambda x); P(X>x)=e^(-lambda x)||Mean is 1/lambda and variance is 1/lambda squared
Hypergeometric Distribution||hypergeometric distribution,sampling without replacement||A hypergeometric distribution counts successes when sampling without replacement from a finite population||P(X=k)=C(K,k)C(N-K,n-k)/C(N,n)||Trials are dependent because the population changes
Uniform Distribution||uniform distribution,equally likely outcomes||A uniform distribution gives equally likely outcomes over a finite set or a constant density over an interval||P(outcome)=1/n or f(x)=1/(b-a)||Equal length intervals have equal probability
Hypothesis Testing||hypothesis testing,hypothesis test,null hypothesis||Hypothesis testing compares a null hypothesis with an alternative using a test statistic and significance level||reject null when p-value is below alpha||It controls error rates rather than proving a claim
P-Value||p-value,p value,significance probability||A p-value is the probability of data at least as extreme as observed assuming the null hypothesis is true||p=P(data as extreme or more extreme given null)||A small p-value argues against the null
Confidence Interval||confidence interval,interval estimate,margin of error||A confidence interval gives a range of plausible values for a population parameter||x-bar plus or minus z times standard error||A 95% method captures the true parameter in about 95 percent of repeated samples
Type I Error||type i error,false positive,rejecting a true null||A Type I error rejects a true null hypothesis||P(Type I error)=alpha||Alpha is the chosen significance level
Type II Error||type ii error,false negative,missing a real effect||A Type II error fails to reject a false null hypothesis||P(Type II error)=beta||Power is 1 minus beta
Statistical Power||statistical power,power of a test,detecting an effect||Statistical power is the probability of rejecting a false null hypothesis, so it detects a real effect||power=1-beta||Power rises with sample size and effect size
Regression||regression,linear regression,predicting from a relationship||Regression models the relationship between a response variable and predictors to describe and predict outcomes||y = intercept + slope x + error||Least squares fits the line and residuals check the fit
Sampling Distribution||sampling distribution,distribution of a sample statistic||A sampling distribution is the distribution of a sample statistic over repeated samples of fixed size||standard error is the spread of the sampling distribution||The central limit theorem often makes it approximately normal
Probability Distribution||probability distribution,distribution of a random variable||A probability distribution assigns probabilities to the possible values of a random variable||discrete uses a PMF and continuous uses a density||It fully describes the random variable's behaviour
